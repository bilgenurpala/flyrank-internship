# Sitemap Pressure Test

## Prompt

> This is my proof statement: I build the backend that makes AI agents act on real systems — agents that run tools, verify their own work against tests, and roll back or ask a human when something breaks. I am proving this to a technical decision-maker in an AI product team who evaluates the potential of junior engineers, and I want that person to book a conversation with me.
>
> This is my proposed sitemap: Home, Work, About, Contact. Does this sitemap walk that person from landing, to believing me, to taking the one action, and does it actually prove my claim? Tell me which pages earn their place, which are wasted, and what is missing. Be specific, not encouraging.

## Pressure-Test Output

Structurally it's sound — Home / Work / About / Contact is almost exactly the program's suggested default, and there's nothing wrong with the default. But that's also the risk: a default sitemap tends to prove a default claim. Yours isn't "junior backend engineer." It's narrow — agents that run tools, verify against tests, and roll back or escalate. The whole job of this sitemap is making sure it proves that, not the generic version of you.

Home earns its place, but push it harder. Your claim is that an agent acts — the fastest proof is watching it act. The single most convincing thing you have is the SafeBump moment: upgrade breaks a test → automatic rollback → recorded reason. If a technical evaluator has to click into "Work" to find that, you've buried your best card. That moment (or a tight cut of it) belongs on Home, near the top.

Work earns its place — it's the heart — but it carries your biggest hidden risk: dilution. SafeBump proves your specific claim. BE-01…04 and the Foundry RAG prove a broader, weaker one ("competent with backends"). As co-equal tiles in a gallery, a visitor averages them and reads "solid junior," not "builds agents that act on real systems." Make SafeBump the clear headliner — its own deep case with the decision loop, the honesty layer, and eval results — and explicitly demote BE-01…04 to supporting evidence.

About earns its place but is the page most likely to rot into filler. For this audience it isn't biography — it's trajectory (backend → agent engineering) and how you work: human-in-the-loop, honest about what your tests don't cover. That mirrors SafeBump's honesty layer — a real through-line worth making explicit.

Contact earns its place as the destination, but don't trap the action there. "Book a conversation" is your one action — the booking CTA should be reachable from Home and from the SafeBump case, at the moment belief peaks, not only on a page someone has to navigate to.

Wasted: none outright. Cutting Skills and Blog was the right call; keep resisting them. The real waste risk is inside Work (co-equal cases) and inside About (bio bloat), not a whole page.

Missing: (1) the honest build story — one real win, one real limitation — probably living inside the SafeBump case, since for a claim built on "rolls back / asks a human / admits what it couldn't verify," honesty is itself the proof; (2) the credibility links (GitHub, CV, LinkedIn, demo video, booking link) — not pages, but they need a definite home in the Home hero and footer.

## Decision and Change

**Change 1 — SafeBump becomes the unmistakable headliner.** Work is no longer a gallery of co-equal projects. SafeBump is a featured deep case (decision loop, honesty layer, eval results); BE-01…04 and the Foundry Local RAG are explicitly demoted to supporting evidence ("proof of foundations, not co-stars"). This directly answers the pressure test's core warning: co-equal cases average down to "solid junior" and bury the specific claim.

**Change 2 — the SafeBump moment moves to Home.** A tight version of "upgrade breaks a test → automatic rollback → recorded reason" goes near the top of Home, so the evaluator sees the one thing that separates me without hunting for it.

**Change 3 — the booking CTA is no longer trapped on Contact.** It is reachable from Home and from the SafeBump case, at the point where belief peaks.

**Held from before:** Skills and Blog stay excluded from launch. About is scoped to trajectory + how I work (human-in-the-loop, honest about test coverage), not a generic bio.
