# Proof Statement

## Claim, Person, and Action

I build the backend that makes AI agents act on real systems — agents that run tools, verify their own work against tests, and roll back or ask a human when something breaks. I am proving this to a technical decision-maker on an AI product team who evaluates the potential of junior engineers. My lead case is SafeBump, the agent I am building as my capstone: it upgrades a project's dependencies one at a time, proves each upgrade against the project's own test suite, and decides on its own whether to keep it, roll it back, or escalate to a human — running only on a git branch and asking for approval before anything is pushed. My backend engineering projects — FastAPI services with authentication, a database layer, and a containerized stack — show I can build the real systems such an agent runs against. My goal is for that person to book an online or in-person conversation with me.

## Why This Portfolio Needs to Exist

A CV can list the AI systems I build, but it cannot let someone inspect the decisions, architecture, grounding, failure handling, and end-to-end behavior that make those systems trustworthy. Anyone can claim "I build AI agents"; a portfolio is where I show the agent verifying its own work and knowing when to stop.

## Focus Check

- One claim: I build the backend for AI agents that act on real systems, verify their own work, and know when to escalate to a human
- One person: a technical decision-maker on an AI product team
- One action: book a conversation
- Evidence: working systems, architecture, evaluation results, trade-offs, and failure handling
