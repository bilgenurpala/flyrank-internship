# AI Workflow Audit

## Context

Until 27 July, I was completing projects and assignments for three concurrent internships. From 20 July onward, I also began meeting the requirements of the Huawei Student Bootcamp while continuing my FlyRank internship. I work toward daily outcomes rather than fixed focus intervals: on Monday mornings I map the week in my monthly planner, split projects into concrete tasks, and adapt the plan when the work changes. Pilates and cycling help me sustain this workload.

## Three Reusable Target Tasks

### 1. Project Planning and Task Decomposition

**Definition of done:** Before implementation starts, the project has a sequenced plan with concrete deliverables, dependencies, acceptance checks, and a realistic daily scope. I can explain why each step exists and adjust the plan when evidence changes.

### 2. Learning an Unfamiliar Technical Topic

**Definition of done:** I can explain the concept in my own words, apply it in a working example, answer basic follow-up questions without copying an AI response, and add a concise reusable note to Notion.

### 3. Code Review and Debugging

**Definition of done:** The defect is reproducible, its root cause is stated clearly, the smallest appropriate correction is applied, relevant tests pass, and I can explain why the fix works and what regression it prevents.

## Current AI Collaboration Model

Claude is my primary AI workspace. I create one project-level conversation that acts as a mentor and shared project memory, then use separate conversations for each workstream and day. I ask for short explanations of unfamiliar concepts and for the reasoning behind suggested code. I keep implementation and final decisions under my control and convert what I learn into my own Notion knowledge base.
