## Coverage gaps

- **Pack §7 (Source coverage) is unused, and it directly bears on C16.** §7 attributes the architecture and JSON-RPC examples to Source 1, the control hierarchy table to Source 2, and the travel scenario and user interaction models to Source 3. The draft says nothing in the note can be traced without returning to the originals — true at the individual-claim level, but source-level attribution does exist for the major content blocks. **Should be added**, as a qualification to the traceability complaint rather than a retraction of it.
- **The "Automatic vs. Explicit Triggering" distinction (§4) is half-used.** The draft carries the explicit-invocation side for prompts but omits the paired claim that tools can be invoked "automatically" by models during a conversation. **Should be added** — it is the sharpest statement of the trigger asymmetry, which is the note's central thesis.
- **Logging's deprecation (§6).** **Should be added** (see C10).
- **The pack's own confidence labels (§3).** All five supported claims are marked "Confidence: High." The draft never mentions that the pack self-assesses. **Optional** — one line at most, and it is the pack's assessment, not an independent one.
- **Prompt examples "Summarize my meetings" and "Draft an email" (§5).** These show prompts as user-facing commands more clearly than `plan-vacation` alone. **Optional.**
- **Tool examples `calculator_arithmetic`, `weather_current`, `sendEmail` (§5).** **Not needed** — the note already has sufficient tool illustration.

## Critique limitations

This audit compares the Draft Note to the Evidence Pack only. It cannot verify:

- **Whether any quoted passage is verbatim, complete, or correctly attributed.** NotebookLM stripped the citation markers on export, and Pack §3 compounds this: every **Source Reference** field is blank. So for the five supported claims there is no source-level attribution either — §7 attributes content blocks to sources, but no individual passage in §3 can be tied to Source 1, 2, or 3 without opening them. C6 is the visible symptom; there may be others I cannot detect, because a fabricated or spliced passage would look identical to a faithful one at this layer.
- **Whether `subscriptions/listen`, `*/list`, `*/get`, or `tools/call` are real protocol method names.** I flagged `list_changed` because it is absent from the pack, not because I verified the others are present in the sources.
- **Whether protocol version 2026-07-28 exists, and whether Source 2 is genuinely older in content rather than merely older in stated version.** The pack's age-based explanation of the Sampling/Elicitation discrepancy (C9) is itself unverifiable here, and so is the deprecation of Sampling and Logging.
- **Whether the pack omitted material from the sources.** Every absence claim in the draft (C17) — no transport, no auth, no pagination, no performance data — is a claim about *the pack*, not about the sources. The sources may cover all of it. The draft does not make this distinction, and I cannot close the gap from inside this document.
- **Whether the travel scenario as the pack states it (C11) matches Source 3's actual scenario,** or whether the pack itself already composed it from scattered examples.
